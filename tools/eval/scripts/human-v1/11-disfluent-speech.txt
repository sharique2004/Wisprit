# category: disfluent-speech

# How people actually talk when they are dictating and thinking at the same
# time: filled pauses, false starts, doubled words, and a mid-sentence change of
# mind. Written out rather than improvised so every speaker produces the same
# disfluencies and the numbers stay comparable across the three passes.
#
# The line is what you SAY. The `ref=` is what the document should end up
# containing — filler gone, restarts resolved, the abandoned half of a self-
# correction dropped. That gap IS the measurement: the raw ASR row scores
# terribly here by design, and the drop from raw to final is what the cleanup
# stages are worth.
#
# ds-04, ds-11 and ds-13 to ds-16 are the self-correction cases and they are the
# hard ones. The target keeps the LAST thing you said, not the first. A pipeline
# that keeps both has produced a sentence that means something different.
#
# ds-16 is the opposite assertion and it has no `ref=` on purpose: a weekday and
# a clock time are not the same kind of thing, so "Thursday, I mean half past
# three" is a clarification and not a replacement, and the whole line has to
# survive. Read it as a clarification, not as a correction — if you say it with
# the falling, cancelling intonation of ds-04 you are recording a different
# sentence from the one the reference describes.
#
# Pace: read them exactly as written, at speaking speed, and let the "um" be a
# real "um" — a crisply articulated filler is not the same acoustic event as a
# real one and does not test the same thing.
# Distance: 20-30 cm.
