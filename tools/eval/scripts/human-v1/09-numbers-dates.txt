# Digits, dates, times, currency, percentages and units — the inverse text
# normalization the deterministic post-process is supposed to do.
#
# Read the digit forms the way you would naturally say them: "14th" is
# "fourteenth", "3:15" is "three fifteen", "$1,200" is "twelve hundred dollars"
# or "one thousand two hundred dollars", whichever is natural for you. That is
# why these lines need no `ref=` — the written form IS the target, and your
# spoken form is the input.
#
# `.asr` normalization folds cardinals 0-999 and ordinals 1st-31st both ways
# (docs/eval/DEFINITIONS.md), so a WER here is not punishing "17" vs
# "seventeen". CER is, which is the point: this is the formatter's category.
#
# Pace: normal. Do not group digits helpfully; say them the way you say them.
